How to read this
Sales did not fall when rank 4's share halved: acquisitions went from 18.3 to 20.1 a week and conversion from 8.0% to 8.6%. Ranks 2 and 3 absorbed most of the shift, and they convert about as well as rank 4. The cost shows up only in rank 1.

Spearman rank correlations with 20,000-shuffle permutation p-values and bootstrap ranges, n = 14 full weeks. Lead volume and rank shares both trended over the same weeks, so 14 weeks can't fully separate “more leads” from “the top rank got smaller”. Fourteen relationships were tested. The two lead results above stay significant after a Bonferroni correction (threshold 0.0036).

04 · Conversion by rank

Only rank 1 converts clearly lower. Ranks 2, 3 and 4 are level.

Acquisitions ÷ contacted over the 14 full weeks: rank 1 6.7%, ranks 2–4 together 9.6%. The gap is 2.9 points (95% range 0.4 to 5.1). So the golden rule in practice is to keep contacted orders out of rank 1, more than to push everything to rank 4.

05 · The last three full weeks

Since 18 Oct the top rank stopped converting.

06 · What this changes

The sweet spot stays. The routing upside gets smaller and more specific.

Stays
100 leads a day (700 a week), never above 150 in one day. The scorecard adds the rep-level reason: when weekly leads rise, the extra contacted orders land on rank 1.
Revised
Routing is worth +3 to +5 acquisitions a month per 10 points of contacted share. Tab 1 said +5 from CRM tenure groups. The scorecard's rank groups give +3 (range 0 to 5).
New rule to test
Hold rank 1 at 10% of contacted orders or less, its level before 20 Sep. It has been 19% since. Estimated gain: about +3 acquisitions a month.

Rule estimate: 9.3 points of contacted share (19.2% − 9.9%) × 2.9-point conversion gap × about 1,000 contacted orders a month = 2.7 acquisitions. For scale, the lead-volume decision in tab 1 moves about 20 acquisitions a month, so volume and cost per lead remain the bigger levers.

07 · Open questions and method

Five things to ask the data team before comparing the two reports.

Open questions
  • Full export. The PDF stops after “Levite Eric” in rank 2. Rep rows for ranks 3 and 4, the per-rep SOV column and the SOV-contribution column are cut off. A CSV or Excel export would let every rep be matched.
  • SOV definition. $403.0K (Jan–Nov) against $836.4K in the CRM (Mar–Nov). CRM SOV is exactly 80% of contract price. What does the scorecard count?
  • Calls definition. 175,774 against 114,024. For the four reps fully inside both reports, the scorecard shows 1.07× to 3.03× the CRM's calls.
  • Rank rule. Which 60-day metric sets the rank, how often it's refreshed, and each rep's rank by week. Rank 3 sits below rank 2 on SOV per contacted and on conversion.
  • Week numbering. The charts' “week 45” is 8–10 Nov. The charts also cover weeks 31–45 only, while the tables cover January to November.
Method
  • Source: Employee Rank French+Longevity, Sisense export stamped 10 Nov 2026 15:00, filter “This & last 10 months”. French only. The two uploaded PDFs are identical files.
  • Weekly values were read from the chart's vector paths in the PDF, not by eye. All 67 printed data labels match, and rank shares sum to 100% in every week.
  • Week alignment was tested eight ways. A Sunday-start week labeled one behind ISO fits the CRM at r = 0.95. A Monday start fits at 0.93. No shift fits at 0.42 or lower.
  • Week 45 (8–10 Nov) is partial. It's shown in charts and left out of every statistic.
  • Rank conversion = Σ(CRM contacted × rank share × rank rate) ÷ Σ(CRM contacted × rank share), with a 10,000-sample bootstrap across weeks.
  • Before/after test: two-proportion z-test on the estimated counts.
