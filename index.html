sells the most?

01 · Why there is a sweet spot

The team has a call ceiling. More leads mean fewer calls per lead.

Daily leads rose 3.7× (48 → 177), but daily calls rose only 1.4× (482 → 670). As a result, calls per lead fell from 10.0 to 3.8. After controlling for month and weekday, each +100 leads a day removes 6.2 calls per lead (p < 0.001) and 8.3 points of contact rate (p = 0.003).

02 · Where sales peak

Acquisitions per day climb until ~130 leads, then drop.

A Poisson regression controlling for month and weekday puts the peak at 120 leads a day (bootstrap middle 50%: 99–138). This curve is a supporting signal, not proof. After the controls, the curve term is not significant on its own (p = 0.13), so the decision rests on the band results: 150+ days are clearly worse.

Method
  • Sources: CRM By-Day (1 Mar–11 Nov), By-Rep, By-Course and mCost (13 months and 30 days). 11 Nov is partial and excluded. The data team's Employee Rank scorecard (1 Jan–10 Nov) is compared in tab 2.
  • Open weekday = Mon–Fri with calls logged (182 days). 14 weekend days had no calls (closed).
  • Peak: Poisson GLM, acqs ~ leads + leads² + month + weekday, robust SE, 1,000-sample bootstrap.
  • 150+ test: two-proportion z-test on acquisitions ÷ contacted (25/479 vs 217/2,506), plus Mann–Whitney on acquisitions per day (p = 0.059).
  • Controls: month fixed effects absorb team changes and seasonality. Weekday effects absorb the Monday backlog.
Not in the uploaded data: needed to turn “per rep, per shift” from an estimate into a measurement
  • Shift and hours log (who worked, which hours, each day). The previous dashboard flagged the same gap. Per-shift figures here assume 7 reps on shift.
  • By-Rep × By-Day export (contacted, calls and acquisitions per rep per day). Without it, the golden-rule share can't be measured for each day.
  • Last-30-day By-Rep export. The uploaded rep file covers all 8 months, so the “top reps in the last 30 days” ranking uses the full period.
  • Longevity files (By-Day 49, by-Course 3) cover 2–11 Nov only (128 leads, 8 acquisitions). They're too short to model and are kept out of this analysis.
